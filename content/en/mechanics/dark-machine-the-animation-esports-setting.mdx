## Golden Valley: The City That Runs on a Game

### A special economic zone, not a host city

Real esports cities compete to host events. Golden Valley inverts that relationship. It is described as the only city in Japan designated as a special economic zone built around the game, using DARK MACHINE to drive the local economy rather than simply renting out an arena for a weekend.

That distinction does a lot of quiet work. A host city can lose a tournament and move on. A city whose identity, funding, and reputation are tied to one competitive title cannot. Every match becomes a referendum on the place itself, and every roster decision becomes local news.

### What the zone does for the story

- **A home team to root for (and against).** Golden Valley gives the cast a hometown with a reputation to defend.
- **Visible stakes.** Instead of an abstract prize pool, the reward is the health of a region.
- **Built-in tension with the outside.** A special designation invites envy, scrutiny, and rivals who want the same status.
- **Geography as drama.** Where a character trains, lives, and competes all sits inside one economically charged footprint.

This is the piece of the Dark Machine The Animation esports setting that most rewards attention. The show is not asking whether its heroes can win a trophy. It is asking whether a city can keep betting its future on a video game.
