A simple rule: if a claim cannot be traced back to an official channel or a news outlet that cites one, treat it as unverified. That single habit eliminates most bad information.

### The Verification Chain

When you see a claim about an episode — a title, a delay, an extra episode — ask three questions. Who said it first? Do they cite a primary source? Has a second independent outlet repeated it? If the answer to the last two is no, file it under "possible" rather than "confirmed." This is the same discipline professional entertainment writers use, and it costs you nothing to apply it.

## Reading an Episode Listing Like a Pro

Anime listings use a vocabulary that trips up newcomers and veterans alike. Understanding it makes any **Dark Machine The Animation episode guide** far easier to read.

| Term | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Simulcast | Same-day streaming release close to the Japanese broadcast |
| Cour | A broadcast block, usually around 12–13 episodes |
| Split cour | A season that pauses and resumes later |
| Recap episode | A compilation episode that does not advance the story |
| Special / OVA | Bonus episode released outside the main broadcast run |
| Delayed broadcast | A postponed airing, often due to production or scheduling |
| Sub / dub | Subtitled versus dubbed audio versions, which release on different timelines |

Two of these cause the most confusion. First, recap episodes are sometimes inserted mid-season and are frequently mistaken for new story content — if a synopsis reads like a summary of prior events, it probably is. Second, a split cour can make a series look like it ended when it has simply paused. Knowing the difference prevents a lot of unnecessary disappointment.
