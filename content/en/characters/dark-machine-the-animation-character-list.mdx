## Building Your Own Dark Machine The Animation Character List Tracker

Rather than refreshing five tabs a day, build a single document you control. A spreadsheet with seven columns handles almost everything.

| Column | Example entry format | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Character name | Full name, plus romanization if known | Prevents duplicate entries |
| Role tier | Protagonist / antagonist / support | Sets expectations |
| Voice actor | Name, with language noted | Tracks cast reveals |
| First appearance | Episode or trailer timestamp | Anchors the entry |
| Source | Official site, PV, credits, community report | Lets you audit later |
| Status | Confirmed / credited / unverified | Flags what needs checking |
| Notes | One-line description of function | Keeps the roster readable |

### Four steps to set it up

- **Create the file before the first trailer.** Adding entries as they land is far easier than reconstructing a roster after episode six.
- **Log the source every single time.** An unsourced row is a row you will eventually delete.
- **Use the Status column ruthlessly.** Anything not from an official channel or on-screen credit stays "unverified" until proven otherwise.
- **Review after each episode.** Two minutes of cleanup beats an hour of untangling duplicates later.

Community reports are genuinely useful here — fans spot background faces, decode credit scrolls, and catch romanization quirks faster than any single writer. Just label those rows as community reports so future-you knows the difference between a confirmed credit and a sharp-eyed observation.

## Character Archetypes to Watch For in an Ensemble Premise

Even without names, you can anticipate the shape of the cast. Ensemble series lean on recognizable archetypes because they let a large group read clearly in a short episode count.

| Archetype | Narrative function | Clue to watch in early material |

| The viewpoint lead | Audience entry point | Most screen time in the first PV |

| The hidden antagonist | Reframes the story late | Named but barely shown |
| The mentor | Delivers the rules of the world | Older design, minimal action shots |
| The specialist | Supplies a needed skill | Distinct visual motif or equipment |

A second rule of thumb worth keeping: a single-cour TV season rarely gives meaningful screen time to more than about eight to twelve named characters. If a fan-made **Dark Machine The Animation character list** balloons past two dozen names before broadcast, most of those entries will be background figures.

## Common Mistakes Fans Make With Early Character Lists

- **Treating a teaser silhouette as a confirmed character.** A silhouette is a design tease, not a roster entry.
- **Merging two romanizations of one name.** Japanese names often produce two or three plausible English spellings; pick one and note the alternates.
- **Assuming a voice actor's other roles predict this one.** Casting against type is common and makes for great surprises.
- **Copying another site's list wholesale.** Errors propagate. Verify against a trailer or credit scroll before importing.
- **Deleting entries instead of archiving them.** Strike through an entry rather than erasing it — sometimes a "cut" character reappears with a new name.

Keeping a clean, sourced **Dark Machine The Animation character list** is ultimately a small act of patience. The information is coming; the only question is whether your notes will be ready when it does.
